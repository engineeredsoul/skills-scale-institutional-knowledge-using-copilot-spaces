# OctoAcme Project Management Documentation

Welcome to the OctoAcme project management process documentation. This folder contains comprehensive guides for running projects at OctoAcme, designed to standardize workflows, clarify roles, and ensure consistent delivery across all cross-functional initiatives.

## Overview of OctoAcme Project Management

OctoAcme follows a structured, iterative project lifecycle built on five core phases: **Initiation**, **Planning**, **Execution**, **Release**, and **Retrospective**. The organization prioritizes customer value, data-driven decisions, and psychological safety through a clear project governance model. Each project is anchored by a lightweight Project One-pager that defines the business problem, success metrics, and stakeholder alignment before moving into detailed planning. This gate-based approach ensures that only well-validated initiatives move forward, reducing rework and misalignment downstream.

The team operates with three primary personas working in concert: **Product Managers** who define what should be built and own success metrics; **Project Managers** who coordinate delivery, manage risks, and maintain stakeholder communication; and **Developers** who implement features, write tests, and collaborate on design decisions. Communication happens through a consistent cadence—daily standups focus on progress and blockers, weekly syncs between PM and Product Lead drive alignment, and twice-weekly delivery standups keep the team synchronized. Risk and dependencies are actively managed through a risk register, escalation paths spanning from team-level to sponsor-level, and regular reviews during weekly syncs to prevent surprises.

Quality and delivery excellence are embedded throughout execution. The team uses GitHub Projects for workflow visibility (Backlog → Ready → In Progress → In Review → QA → Done), enforces small pull requests (≤400 lines), requires automated testing and security scanning in CI, and maintains a clear Definition of Done before work is considered complete. Releases follow a standardized process with pre-deployment checklists, smoke tests, and rollback plans to reduce production risk. After each sprint, release, or milestone, the team conducts retrospectives to capture learnings and convert them into prioritized action items, creating a culture of continuous improvement and accountability.
